Safety rules

Science is only fun when nobody gets hurt. Read this once, then remember it every time.

The six rules

  • A grown-up is always in the room. Not in the next room. In this one.
  • Read the whole experiment first, including the safety box, before you touch anything.
  • Nothing goes in your mouth. Not even if it is made of food. Not to taste, not to check.
  • Grown-ups handle hot water, flames, ovens and sharp things. Every time, no exceptions.
  • Protect your eyes. Goggles or old swimming goggles for anything that fizzes, sprays or might splash.
  • Tidy up and wash your hands when you have finished, and pour leftovers away as the experiment tells you.

Where to work

  • Clear the table first and cover it with newspaper or an old tray.
  • Tie long hair back and roll your sleeves up.
  • Keep drinks and food right out of the room, so nothing gets mixed up.
  • Do the messy ones outside or in the sink if you can.
  • Know where the tap and a cloth are before you start, not after the spill.

If something goes wrong

  • In your eyes: rinse with clean cool water for 10 minutes and tell a grown-up straight away.
  • On your skin: rinse it off under the tap.
  • Swallowed: tell a grown-up immediately. Do not try to make yourself sick.
  • Spilled on the floor: stop, tell somebody, and wipe it up before anyone walks through it.
  • Anything worse: a grown-up should ring 111 for advice, or 999 in an emergency.

For grown-ups

Every experiment on this site lists an age guide, a difficulty level, and the specific hazards involved. The ones needing hot water, heat, or rubbing alcohol are marked with an adult in the age guide, and the safety box on each page spells out exactly why.

Please read the experiment through before your child starts, and take charge of the steps marked for adults yourself. The age guides assume you are supervising throughout. Household chemicals should never be mixed together outside of these instructions — in particular, never combine bleach with vinegar, ammonia, or any other cleaner.

Nothing on this site needs specialist equipment. If an experiment calls for something you would rather not have in the house, skip it — there are plenty of others.
